# Assignment 1: Task Division (Trio 1)

Three people, three equal shares of roughly 900 to 1000 words each, against a 3000-word limit.
Roles are **Person A, Person B and Person C**. Pick who takes which role together.

## Step 0, together: choose the project

Nothing can be drafted before the trio (and the group, see the root proposal) agrees on **one
data science project** and writes down its facts: what data it collects, from whom, what it does
with them, and who builds and runs it. Every section is an analysis of those facts, so this
step comes first and is done jointly.

## The split

| Role | Owns | Advice share (Section IV) | Approx. words |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Person A** | **Section I, Introduction + Description.** The project; which personal data are processed; whether special categories apply; the actor roles (data subject, controller, processor), with reasoning for the controller/processor determination | Alternatives to personal data: anonymisation, pseudonymisation, or using non-personal data instead | 500 + 250 = 750, **plus integrator** |
| **Person B** | **Section II, How are the data processed?** The purpose of the processing, then each data protection principle applied to the project: purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity and confidentiality, accountability | Technical and organisational measures: security, retention periods, records of processing, DPIA, data protection by design | 550 + 300 = 850 |
| **Person C** | **Section III, On which legal basis?** Which Article 6 ground the processing relies on, its criteria applied to the project, why it works or where relying on it is problematic (and Article 9 if special categories apply) | The overall compliance conclusion: compliant or not, and the way forward | 900 + 150 = 1050 |

**Why it balances:** Person C carries the heaviest section, but writes the least advice. Person A
has the lightest writing load, so also takes the **integrator** role: the report header (group
number, names, student numbers, word count), footnote formatting, the final word count, the
Technology Statement, and rendering the final PDF from the .qmd.

## Where to start now

The lectures already given cover all three analytical sections:

| Role | Lecture material to start from |
|---|---|
| Person A | Lecture 1: scope and concepts of the GDPR, personal data, controllers and processors |
| Person B | Lecture 3: data protection principles (Article 5) |
| Person C | Lecture 2: rules on lawful processing (Article 6, consent, legitimate interest) |

Each section owner is responsible for the **legal provisions and case law** cited in their own
section. The rubric puts 9 of the 15 points on analysis and assessment, and it asks for correct
provisions and case law to support it.

## Review and integration

1. **Peer review in a ring.** A reviews B, B reviews C, C reviews A. Check the legal reasoning
   and the facts, not just the language.
2. **Section IV sitting.** All three together: combine the three advice shares into one
   conclusion that follows logically from Sections I to III. The rubric gives 4 points to advice.
3. **Cross-trio review.** Trio 2 reads the full draft (see the root proposal).
4. **Integration** by Person A: one voice, one citation style, under 3000 words excluding
   footnotes, header complete, Technology Statement filled in.

## AI rule, for everyone

AI Index Level 2: AI may only review a finished draft for spelling, grammar, coherence and
referencing. It never writes text, tables or graphs. Whoever uses a tool reports it to the
integrator for the Technology Statement.
